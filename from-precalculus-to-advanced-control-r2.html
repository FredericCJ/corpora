04 / Specialist shelves

Fourteen destinations. Concrete outcomes.

Each shelf states the skills to bring, the material to focus on, and a task that makes understanding observable. “Core” means a recommended entry or principal reference for that field. It does not rank every book by difficulty or require every core title to be completed.

FIELD 01

Differential geometry & Lie theory

Core shelf[20] [27]
Advanced / companionChoose by question
Entry skills, focus & mastery task

Bring these skills

  • Write proofs with quantifiers, maps, and equivalence relations.
  • Use linear maps, dual spaces, and matrix exponentials.
  • Differentiate multivariable maps and recognize local invertibility.
  • Use basic metric/topological ideas for the manifold route.

Study focus

  • Charts, tangent/cotangent spaces, and coordinate changes.
  • Vector fields, flows, differential forms, and Lie brackets.
  • Matrix Lie groups, Lie algebras, exponential maps, and group actions.
  • Separate the concrete matrix-group entry from the general manifold treatment.

Produce this evidence

Develop a worked SO(3) example: derive its tangent algebra, compute a matrix exponential, transform a tangent vector between coordinates, and explain where an inverse coordinate representation ceases to be unique. State the local and global claims separately.

Watch for: Treating a coordinate chart or the exponential map as a globally unique parametrization of the entire state space.

FIELD 02

Geometric control theory

Core shelf[37]
Advanced / companion[46]
Entry skills, focus & mastery task

Bring these skills

  • Calculate flows and brackets of vector fields.
  • Understand distributions and basic manifold language.
  • Apply linear controllability tests and state-space modeling.
  • State existence assumptions for a controlled ODE.

Study focus

  • Accessibility, controllability, and orbit constructions.
  • Bracket-generating distributions and nonholonomic motion.
  • Control-affine systems and feedback transformations.
  • Geometric optimality conditions and their hypotheses.

Produce this evidence

Model a driftless unicycle, compute its control vector fields and their Lie bracket with an explicit sign convention, establish the applicable rank condition, and construct a small motion sequence that demonstrates the bracket direction. Explain which controllability conclusion the theorem permits.

Watch for: Applying a Lie-algebra rank condition without checking drift, admissible controls, and the distinction between accessibility and controllability.

FIELD 03

Mathematical control theory

Core shelf[29]
Advanced / companion[38]
Entry skills, focus & mastery task

Bring these skills

  • Manipulate linear systems, Gramians, and realizations.
  • Use ODE existence, continuity, and stability arguments.
  • Handle norms, convergence, and mathematical proofs.
  • Add function-space concepts when an infinite-dimensional chapter needs them.

Study focus

  • Reachability, observability, and realization.
  • Stabilization and feedback structure.
  • Admissible controls, solution concepts, and theorem hypotheses.
  • Understand precisely what changes beyond finite dimension.

Produce this evidence

Construct a state-space example that is stabilizable but not controllable, and one that is detectable but not observable. Prove each property, design the permitted controller/observer, and explain why a transfer-function-only description can hide an internal mode.

Watch for: Interchanging controllability with stabilizability, or input-output stability with internal stability.

FIELD 04

Nonlinear systems & control

Core shelf[23] [33]
Advanced / companion[31]
Entry skills, focus & mastery task

Bring these skills

  • Linearize a physical model about an equilibrium.
  • Use matrix spectra and quadratic forms.
  • Understand ODE solutions and invariant sets.
  • Differentiate scalar functions along trajectories.

Study focus

  • Lyapunov and invariance arguments with explicit domains.
  • Local, global, asymptotic, exponential, and input-to-state stability.
  • Passivity, feedback linearization, and zero dynamics.
  • Controller design under saturation, uncertainty, and unmodeled dynamics.

Produce this evidence

For a nonlinear pendulum or flexible-joint model, find equilibria, compare linearization with a Lyapunov argument, derive a certified region where possible, and simulate initial conditions both inside and outside it. Report what the proof establishes beyond the plots.

Watch for: Promoting a local result or successful simulations into a claim of global stability.

FIELD 05

Geometric mechanics

Core shelf[28]
Advanced / companion[36] [45]
Entry skills, focus & mastery task

Bring these skills

  • Derive Newton-Euler and Euler-Lagrange equations.
  • Use multivariable calculus and variational arguments.
  • Understand tangent/cotangent bundles and differential forms for the geometric chapters.
  • Distinguish configuration, velocity, momentum, and state.

Study focus

  • Lagrangian and Hamiltonian descriptions.
  • Symplectic structure, symmetry, and momentum maps.
  • Reduction and conservation laws.

Derive a mechanical model in Lagrangian and Hamiltonian coordinates, verify a conserved quantity for the unforced case, and then add a nonholonomic constraint. Explain why its constrained equations require a different argument from unconstrained stationary-action equations.

FIELD 06

Optimal control

Core shelf[30]
Advanced / companion[39]
Entry skills, focus & mastery task

Bring these skills

  • Formulate state-space dynamics and boundary conditions.
  • Use gradients, constrained extrema, and ODEs.
  • Understand quadratic forms and linear feedback.
  • Add rigorous analysis for existence and sufficiency arguments.

Study focus

  • Costs, admissible controls, and endpoint conditions.
  • Calculus of variations and the maximum principle.
  • Dynamic programming and the Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman equation.
  • LQR, constrained controls, and necessary versus sufficient conditions.

Produce this evidence

Solve a finite-horizon linear-quadratic problem by Riccati integration and a small direct transcription. Compare costs and trajectories, state the boundary conditions, and check numerical convergence. Use a constrained variant to identify which unconstrained conclusions stop applying.

Watch for: Assuming that satisfying the maximum principle or a numerical stationarity condition proves global optimality.

FIELD 07

Estimation & stochastic methods

Core shelf[32]
Advanced / companionChoose by question
Entry skills, focus & mastery task

Bring these skills

  • Compute conditional expectation and covariance of random vectors.
  • Use linear state-space models and observability.
  • Understand Gaussian models, independence, and conditioning.
  • Solve well-scaled linear systems without explicit matrix inversion.

Study focus

  • Least squares, Bayesian conditioning, and Kalman filtering.

FIELD 08

Numerical, digital & sampled-data control

Core shelf[17] [19] [25]
Advanced / companion[43] [44]
Entry skills, focus & mastery task

Bring these skills

  • Analyze continuous-time linear feedback and state-space models.
  • Use difference equations, transforms, and matrix exponentials.
  • Distinguish floating-point error from problem conditioning.
  • Model sampling, holding, and computation timing explicitly.

Study focus

  • Exact and approximate discretization; sampling and aliasing.

Implement one controller in continuous simulation and as a sampled algorithm with a held actuator command. Sweep the sample period and computation delay, add quantization and saturation, and compare sampled states with the full intersample trajectory. Produce a reproducible numerical-error check.

FIELD 09

Model predictive control

Core shelf[54] [55]
Advanced / companion[58] [59] [60]
Entry skills, focus & mastery task

Bring these skills

  • Discretize dynamics and design linear feedback.
  • Formulate constrained QPs and interpret KKT conditions.
  • Use Lyapunov arguments and invariant-set concepts.

Study focus

  • Prediction models, costs, horizons, and constraints.

Core shelf[18] [40] [41]

Three projects that connect the shelves.

The following projects are original study assignments. Reuse a small set of plants so that a new control method can be compared against the same baseline. State the model, units, assumptions and pass criteria before running an experiment.

A
Linear, digital, robust & predictive control

A sampled electromechanical servo

Use an armature-controlled DC motor with current i and angular speed ω as states. Begin with positive physical parameters and an explicitly bounded voltage input.

L di/dt = v − Ri − keω
J dω/dt = kti − bω − τL

Here L is inductance, R resistance, J inertia, b viscous damping, ke the back-EMF constant, kt the torque constant and τL the load torque. These equations define the chosen idealized project model; friction, drive dynamics and sensor behavior can be introduced as explicit extensions. Select physically consistent motor constants and state whether load torque is constant, time varying or estimated. When it is modeled as an input, discretize its disturbance channel as well as the voltage-input channel.

A nonholonomic mobile robot

Use the unicycle model with planar position and heading: ẋ = v cos θ, ẏ = v sin θ, θ̇ = ω. Treat heading as an angle on the circle and distinguish the path geometry from its timing.
