Architecture, Design & Management of Large Simulink/MATLAB Projects

A corpus of 53 verified resources in three explicitly distinct branches — Architecture, Design, and Management — mirroring the branch treatment of the companion software-architecture corpus while remaining entirely independent of it: no shared nodes, no cross-references to that corpus or to the control-theory DAG. No rigor filter is applied: the domain’s actual source types are all admitted — MathWorks documentation, MAB/JMAAB guidelines, MISRA AC documents, practitioner articles, conference/technical papers, and vendor guides. The graph is a directed acyclic graph drawn bottom-up: roots are the most atomic, detail-level resources, and every edge points upward toward material that abstracts those details away.

53 nodes  ·  3 branches + automotive band  ·  65 drawn edges  ·  9 abstraction levels  ·  roots at bottom, edges point up  ·  compiled July 2026 · rev. 2

01Why bottom-up — the stated rationale

“Reasoning about higher-order abstraction requires first understanding the underlying detail. One cannot meaningfully evaluate a model-governance framework, a componentization strategy, or a program-level Model-Based Design rollout without first understanding the block-level and file-level mechanics it governs. Therefore this corpus is ordered bottom-up: the root nodes are the most atomic, practical, detail-level resources — specific modeling conventions, block-level practices, naming and interface standards, and basic configuration-management mechanics — and every edge points upward from these detail-level prerequisites to the higher-level material that abstracts them away.”
stated basis for the bottom-up ordering — quoted verbatim into the corpus, as required
  • Three branches, one convention. Architecture (componentization → interfaces → system-level structure), Design (style atoms → block patterns → typing → code generation → design quality), and Management (version-control mechanics → CI → traceability → governance → program scale) are kept as distinct bands, consistent with the two-branch discipline of the earlier software-architecture corpus — but the two corpora share nothing: this one contains no general software-architecture or control-theory nodes.
  • No science bar. Unlike the companion software-architecture corpus, practitioner-oriented material is first-class here: this is what the domain actually offers, and the MathWorks/MAB/MISRA documents are the primary literature.
  • Citation rule. Every node cites title, author (or issuing organization), and year of last publication; “living” marks continuously revised documentation, cited at its current revision (July 2026).
  • Automotive specialization band. Automotive (and adjacent aerospace-certification) nodes cut across all three branches; they are marked ◆ rather than isolated in a cluster, and the domain-neutral core stays visually primary.

07Caveats & verification notes

  • MAAB → MAB rename confirmed: renamed at v5.0, March 2020 (“MAAB guidelines revised and reintroduced as the MathWorks Advisory Board (MAB) Modeling Guidelines, Version 5.0, Release 2020a”).
  • “MAB v6.0” does not exist in MathWorks’ own numbering — the MAB PDF went 5.0 → 23.2 (R2023b) → date-based 24.x/25.x. “Version 6.0” is a genuine JMAAB version (Model Advisor support R2023b, 2023). Any hypothesized “MAB 6.0 (2022)” is incorrect.
  • MISRA AC line: current is SLSF:2023 2nd ed. + Amendments 1–4 (R2023b–R2025a); GMG:2023 is the required companion. MISRA AC TL (TargetLink) and MISRA AC AGC are historical — MISRA no longer maintains code-generator-specific guidelines; flag MISRA AC TL as legacy if ever added.
  • Point verifications: Aarenstrup 2015 (The MathWorks; free MathWorks-hosted ebook); SAE 2010-01-0938 confirmed with a free MathWorks-hosted PDF; Deissenboeck is ICSE 2008 (pp. 603–612), not 2009; buildtool R2022b and MATLAB Test R2023a confirmed.
  • Deliberately excluded: Higham & Higham MATLAB Guide and Nicolescu & Mosterman Model-Based Design for Embedded Systems — general works not verified to carry large-project/model-architecture content, and a risk to the corpus’s independence rule.
  • Independence maintained: no shared nodes and no cross-references with the companion software-architecture cyclic graph or the earlier control-theory DAG; every node here is Simulink/MATLAB/MBD-specific, or generic tooling as applied to Simulink/MATLAB.
