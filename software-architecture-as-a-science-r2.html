Software Architecture as a Science

A clustered corpus of 77 methodologically rigorous works, organized into two explicitly distinct branches — Branch A: software architecture as artifact and discipline (structures, styles, theory) and Branch B: architecture design as process (decision-making, method, how one arrives at an architecture) — joined by a shared evaluation-and-quality spine. Every edge carries one of nine fixed relation kinds; the graph is deliberately cyclic, with four documented feedback loops drawn in dashed red. Every node carries a full citation (title, author, year of last publication).

77 nodes  ·  14 clusters  ·  95 typed edges  ·  9 relation kinds  ·  4 documented cycles  ·  compiled July 2026 · rev. 2

01Scope & reading rules

Four rules govern admission and representation. They are stated here explicitly because every downstream choice — node set, edge set, drawing — follows from them.

  • Science bar. Only resources that treat architecture and its design as a science are admitted: detailed methodologies, formal methods, and evaluation frameworks. Pattern catalogs, best-practice guides, and anecdotal practitioner literature are excluded. Three borderline admissions survive review and are flagged in the references — Rozanski–Woods [11], Weyns [33], and Clements–Northrop [38] — admitted as structured reference/consolidation nodes, not as formal theory.
  • Two branches, explicitly distinct. Branch A collects work about the artifact and discipline: what an architecture is, how it is described, formalized, verified, and evolved. Branch B collects work about the process: how architectures are designed, decided, rationalized, and how designers actually reason. The shared spine (evaluation, quality models, economics) is what binds them — evaluation assesses artifacts (A) and drives design (B).
  • “Model” is read in both senses — and both are populated. (a) Formal and graphical modeling: notations, architecture description languages, viewpoint/view frameworks — clusters A2 (views & description), A3 (ADLs & formal semantics), A4 (model checking). (b) Conceptual and evaluation models: quality-attribute frameworks, architecture evaluation methods, design-rationale models — clusters S1 (evaluation canon), S2 (quality-attribute models), B3 (rationale & architectural knowledge). Neither sense is treated as the default; the corpus deliberately includes resources from both.
  • Directed cyclic graph, not a DAG. Resources reference and feed back into one another: a design-process methodology draws on an evaluation model, whose refinement in turn revises the methodology. The relation kind critiques / feeds-back-into is what makes the graph cyclic; cutting its edges would recover a DAG but lose exactly the intellectual feedback this corpus is designed to represent. Four genuine cycles are documented in §05.
  • Citation rule. Every node cites title, author, and year of last publication, edition-verified. Notably: Taylor–Medvidovic–Dashofy has no second edition — the Wiley ©2010 printing is the only one; Bass–Clements–Kazman is cited at its 4th edition (2021); Cervantes–Kazman at its 2nd (2024); ISO/IEC/IEEE 42010 at its 2022 revision; ISO/IEC 25010 at its 2023 revision.

05The four documented cycles

These loops are why the corpus is a directed cyclic graph and not a DAG. Each is drawn in dashed red on the board; if a downstream tool ever requires acyclicity, these are the edges to cut — at the cost of losing the feedback the corpus is designed to represent.

  • CYCLE 1 ADL research ↔ standardization ↔ industry needs (Branch A). Medvidovic–Taylor’s classification [15] maps the ADL space → ISO/IEC/IEEE 42010 [8] standardizes description practice → Malavolta et al.’s industry-needs survey [26] critiques both and re-scopes ADL research priorities → the revised standard feeds back into the classification lineage.
  • CYCLE 2 Architectural mismatch, 1995 ↔ 2009 (Branch A). The original diagnosis [28] is a prerequisite of its own 14-year retrospective [29], which re-evaluates whether the problem persists and feeds back into the original framing.
  • CYCLE 3 Rationale → decisions → knowledge management → rationale (Branch B). Jansen–Bosch recast architecture as a set of design decisions [48] → Tang–Jin–Han add traceability and reuse DRL-style rationale capture [55] → the ten-year AKM retrospective [57] surveys the line and feeds back into the decision-centric view.
  • CYCLE 4 Design method ↔ evaluation (Shared). ADD 3.0 [46] applies quality-attribute scenarios and ATAM evaluation [65] as internal steps → refined tactics and scenarios feed back into ATAM practice → which re-enters ADD. This is the corpus’s clearest instance of a design-process methodology drawing on an evaluation model whose refinement revises the methodology.
