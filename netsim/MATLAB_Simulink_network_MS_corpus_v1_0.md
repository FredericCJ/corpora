## 9. Coverage summary

All six paradigms were swept live. Coverage is **strongest and most self-sufficient in two places**: (a) the MathWorks-official stratum for wireless system-level simulation — the 2023–2026 stack (wirelessNetworkSimulator core + 5G/WLAN/Bluetooth/Satellite toolboxes + Wireless Network Toolbox packaging) is fully documented by vendor pages, and the key anti-fabrication finding is the *packaging migration* (File Exchange add-on → in-product/dedicated toolbox), which any downstream citation must respect; and (b) the co-simulation/NCS paradigm, where a mature, independent, peer-reviewed lineage exists (TrueTime → Jitterbug/JitterTime, PiccSIM, NMLab, Simulink↔OPNET, WirelessHART co-sim) — this is the paradigm most relevant to control-over-network problems and is almost entirely non-vendor literature. The **academic-simulators** paradigm is dominated by one family (Vienna LTE/5G, fully grounded incl. its Springer monograph) plus GTEC and satellite-networking studies that use SatCom Toolbox as the system-of-record. Recall **thins** in: independent peer-reviewed evaluations of the *new* (2023+) MathWorks network-simulation stack (the tooling is young; third-party calibration/validation studies against 3GPP/IEEE references were not found this session and may barely exist yet — a genuine gap, not a search failure); the thesis stratum (one unverified representative); and File Exchange beyond the vendor's own add-on. The discrete-event (SimEvents) paradigm shows the inverse profile of §2: vendor documentation is stable and older, while the peer-reviewed record is concentrated in one research lineage (MathWorks + Cassandras/Boston University, WSC/WODES/DEDS 2006–2019) oriented to CPS/ITS rather than classical packet networks — consistent with the framing that MATLAB/Simulink is a minority tool whose network-M&S value is paradigm-specific (hybrid control+network, coexistence, standards-based wireless KPIs, satellite scenarios) rather than general-purpose packet simulation.
