Unified self-study DAG · rev. 4

From Precalculus to Advanced Control

A single directed acyclic graph of the literature: one root, two tracks (mathematical and engineering), shared optimization infrastructure, and fourteen target fields — now including predictive, robust, adaptive, sliding-mode, digital, networked, and trajectory layers. Every edge is justified by enumerated subtopic overlap; every node carries a full citation. Tinted nodes are core reading; white nodes are advanced literature; ¶ marks canonical survey or tutorial papers admitted as first-class nodes.

67 nodes  ·  14 target fields  ·  102 drawn edges + prose edges  ·  compiled July 2026

03Revision notes

Adjustments made in this revision (rev. 4), after scrutiny of the expanded selection:

  • Åström–Wittenmark, Computer-Controlled Systems promoted from alternative [A2] to full core node [19]. It and Franklin–Powell–Workman [25] form the parallel core pair of the digital shelf, mirroring the Calculus ∥ Concepts & Contexts convention.
  • Utkin 1992 demoted to reference-only alternative [A7]: canonical but poorly suited to self-study. Shtessel–Edwards–Fridman–Levant [42] is the sliding-mode core; Edwards–Spurgeon becomes alternative [A8]; Utkin–Guldner–Shi [50] carries the applied tier.
  • Ioannou–Sun chosen as the adaptive theory core [51] (author-hosted free PDF), with Åström–Wittenmark Adaptive Control [35] as the engineering core. Narendra–Annaswamy and Sastry–Bodson move to alternatives [A9], [A10]; Hovakimyan–Cao L1 to [A11]. Krstić–Kanellakopoulos–Kokotović [56] and Lavretsky–Wise 2nd ed. 2024 [57] form the advanced tier.
  • Three shared infrastructure nodes added — Boyd–Vandenberghe [8], Nocedal–Wright [26], Boyd et al. LMIs [34] — because MPC, robust, and networked control all silently assume them. This keeps the DAG honest about prerequisites; the long right-hand rails in the drawing are these dependencies made visible.
  • Survey papers admitted as first-class nodes (¶) per instruction: Hespanha et al. NCS survey [62], Heemels et al. event-triggered tutorial [66], Schenato et al. lossy-networks foundations [65], Garone et al. reference governors [60]. Shorter key papers are folded into §05c rather than drawn.
  • Borrelli–Bemporad–Morari kept as a separate core node [55] rather than an alternative to Rawlings–Mayne–Diehl [54]: its explicit/hybrid-MPC content is not subsumed.
  • μ-synthesis gets no standalone node — it lives inside Zhou–Doyle–Glover [48] and Skogestad–Postlethwaite [41]; dwell-time and average dwell-time switching live in Liberzon [47]. Packard–Doyle and Lin–Antsaklis surveys are listed in §05c.
  • Chen–Francis retained despite its reference-only character [43]: it simply is the sampled-data lifting theory; flagged as such.
  • Core vs. advanced is now a visual encoding applied retroactively to the original 34 nodes: Vidyasagar, Zabczyk, Liberzon [39], Jurdjevic, Marsden–Ratiu, and Bloch are marked advanced-tier.
  • Open-access flags added to the bibliography where an official free copy exists (author- or publisher-hosted), per the July 2026 research pass; verify links before relying on them.

Carried from rev. 3: Hespanha [22] replaces Brockett as the linear-systems node (Brockett → [A1]); Boyce–DiPrima–Meade [6] fills the Laplace/ODE gap on the engineering spine; Luenberger → Kirk added; Lee + Hall designated the Differential Geometry & Lie Theory pair; known seams (Kirk → Liberzon rigor jump; thin Precalculus → Lay / → Hibbeler edges; Abbott → Axler proof-culture edge) kept deliberately and documented.

04Edge catalog with overlap enumerations

Progressiveness rule: each edge lists the subtopics shared by parent and child; a lineage is progressive when every consecutive pair overlaps densely. not drawn edges exist in the DAG but are omitted from the drawing (transitive display reduction or kept as prose to limit clutter).

Stage I — Foundations
  • Precalculus → Calculus — functions & transformations; trigonometry; exponentials/logarithms; analytic geometry & conics; sequences as a limits preview.
  • Precalculus → Calculus: Concepts & Contexts — same interface; C&C ∥ Calculus are parallel alternatives with near-total mutual overlap.
  • Precalculus → Linear algebra I (Lay) — linear systems & elimination; matrix arithmetic; plane vectors; linear models.
  • Precalculus → Statics & dynamics (Hibbeler) — vectors & trigonometry; components & dot products; force geometry; simultaneous equations.not drawn — via Calculus
  • Calculus → Real analysis I (Abbott) — ε–δ limits; continuity; derivative as limit; Riemann sums → integral; series tests. Same for C&C → Abbott.
  • Calculus → Hibbeler — kinematics via derivatives/integrals; vector-valued functions; curvilinear coordinates; work integrals.
  • Calculus → Engineering ODEs (Boyce–DiPrima–Meade) — integration technique; power series; first-order models; exponential solutions.
  • Calculus → Convex optimization (Boyd–Vandenberghe) — multivariable calculus; gradients & Hessians; Lagrange multipliers → duality.
  • Lay → Boyd–Vandenberghe — linear systems & least squares; positive semidefinite matrices; matrix norms; quadratic forms.
  • Calculus → Taylor — multivariable differentiation; line integrals; ODE fluency.not drawn — via Hibbeler
  • Calculus → Trefethen–Bau — Taylor expansion for error analysis; norms & convergence.not drawn — via Lay
  • Calculus / Lay → Nocedal–Wright — Taylor's theorem for line search & trust regions; convergence rates; factorizations.not drawn — via Trefethen–Bau
  • Lay → Linear algebra II (Axler) — systems & independence; vector spaces; linear maps as matrices; eigen-theory; orthogonality — re-derived abstractly, determinant-free.
  • Lay → Feedback control (Franklin) — state-space ẋ = Ax + Bu; eigenvalues as poles; rank conditions; similarity transformations.
  • Lay → Trefethen–Bau — LU/QR; least squares; eigenproblems; orthogonality — Lay computes, Trefethen–Bau conditions and stabilizes.
  • Lay → Nocedal–Wright — drawn as the lane-I rail: linear algebra of KKT systems; least squares; sparsity.
Stage II — Analysis & algebra trunk
  • Abbott → Rudin PMA — sequences/series; (uniform) continuity; Riemann integration; uniform convergence; compactness on ℝ.
  • Abbott → Axler — proof craft; ℝ/ℂ fields; suprema in norms; polynomial approximation. Weakest trunk edge, kept deliberately.
  • Rudin PMA → Munkres — metric spaces; compactness & connectedness; topological continuity; Baire-style arguments.
  • Rudin PMA → Rudin RCA — Riemann → Lebesgue; limit interchange; completeness; power series → holomorphy.
  • Rudin PMA → HSD — multivariable differentials; contraction mapping / Picard–Lindelöf; norms on ℝⁿ; uniform convergence for flows.
  • Rudin PMA → Khalil — comparison lemma; Gronwall inequalities; Barbălat; norms.not drawn — via HSD
  • Rudin PMA → Liberzon [39] — ε–δ analysis; function-space convergence; existence arguments.not drawn
  • Axler → HSD — spectral/Jordan structure of e^{tA}; diagonalization; invariant ↔ stable/unstable subspaces; operator norms.
  • Axler → Lee — tangent-space linear algebra; dual spaces & 1-forms; multilinear/tensor algebra.
  • Axler → Hespanha — canonical forms; rank; quadratic forms → Lyapunov equations & Gramians; eigenstructure.
  • Axler → Luenberger — inner products; orthogonal projection; adjoints; least squares.
  • Axler → Hall — matrix groups; matrix exponential; complexification; representations.not drawn — via Lee
  • Rudin RCA → Luenberger — Banach/Hilbert; Lᵖ; duality & Hahn–Banach; projections.
  • Rudin RCA → Sontag — measurable / L∞ controls; Carathéodory ODEs; Lᵖ signals.not drawn — via Luenberger
  • Rudin RCA → Papoulis — measure-theoretic probability underpinnings; Lᵖ convergence modes; integration.
  • Rudin RCA → Zhou–Doyle–Glover — Hardy spaces H₂/H∞; inner–outer factorization; operator norms.not drawn — prose
Stage III — Dynamics, geometry, systems
  • HSD → Hespanha — ẋ = Ax via matrix exponential; linear phase portraits & stability; forced systems.
  • HSD → Khalil — equilibria & linearization; phase plane; limit cycles; flow stability.
  • HSD → Vidyasagar — existence/uniqueness; linearization; periodic solutions.
  • HSD → Arnold — Hamiltonian planar systems; periodic orbits; stability; phase portraits.
  • Munkres → Lee — topological/quotient/product spaces; paracompactness; covering spaces.
  • Lee → Hall — manifolds & maps; tangent spaces; flows & Lie brackets; Lie subgroups.
  • Lee → Arnold — (co)tangent bundles; differential forms; flows; Lie derivative.
  • Lee → Agrachev–Sachkov — vector fields & flows; brackets; Frobenius/distributions; orbits.
  • Lee → Marsden–Ratiu — manifolds; forms; Lie derivative.not drawn — via Arnold/Hall
  • Luenberger → Sontag — DP & maximum-principle machinery; Hilbert least squares → LQ; duality.
  • Luenberger → Kirk — variational calculus in normed spaces; constrained optimization; gradient conditions.
  • Hespanha → Sontag — controllability/observability; realization; stabilization; LQR.
  • Hespanha → Kirk — LQR; Riccati; controllability; state feedback.
  • Hespanha → Agrachev–Sachkov — Kalman rank → Lie-algebra rank condition; state-space formalism.
  • Hespanha → Simon — observability Gramians; observers; duality with LQR.
  • Hespanha → Boyd et al. LMIs — Lyapunov equations/inequalities; Riccati ↔ LMI; stability certificates.
  • Boyd–Vandenberghe → LMIs — semidefinite programming; duality; interior-point solvability.
  • Hall → Marsden–Ratiu — adjoint/coadjoint actions; coadjoint orbits ↔ Lie–Poisson; exp map.
  • Hall → Jurdjevic — Lie groups as state spaces; left-invariant systems; exponential coordinates.
  • Trefethen–Bau → Nocedal–Wright — QR/SVD in least squares; conditioning & stability of KKT solves; Krylov methods.
Stage IV — Engineering spine
  • Hibbeler → Franklin — Newton–Euler modeling; FBD → equations of motion; mass–spring–damper plants; mechanism kinematics.
  • Hibbeler → Taylor — particle & rigid-body dynamics; energy/momentum methods; oscillations; rotating frames.
  • BDM → Franklin — linear constant-coefficient ODEs; Laplace transforms & transfer functions; step/impulse response; resonance.
  • BDM → Papoulis — transforms; linear system response; convolution; series methods.
  • BDM → Åström–Wittenmark CCS — differential ↔ difference equations; transforms; discretization of linear dynamics.
  • Taylor → Arnold — Lagrangian & variational principles; conservation laws; Euler rigid body; normal modes. The engineering-track merge into geometric mechanics.
  • Franklin → Kirk — state-space models; specs → cost functionals; LQR; feedback structures.
  • Franklin → Slotine–Li — linear design baseline; tracking & robustness specs; actuator models.
  • Franklin → Simon — observers (Luenberger observer → Kalman filter); state-space models; discretization.
  • Franklin → FPW Digital Control — the same authors' program re-derived under sampling: z-transforms, discrete equivalents, quantization.
  • Franklin → Åström–Wittenmark CCS — transfer-function & state-space design; sampling; z-plane pole placement.
  • Franklin → DFT (Doyle–Francis–Tannenbaum) — loop shaping; sensitivity & complementary sensitivity; stability margins → formal H∞ statement.
  • Franklin → Biagiotti–Melchiorri — servo loops consuming references; feedforward; motion profiles as reference inputs.
  • Franklin → Åström–Wittenmark Adaptive — pole placement & self-tuning loops; digital implementation; gain scheduling.
  • Papoulis → ÅW Adaptive — recursive least squares; stochastic models; identification.
  • Papoulis → Simon — random vectors & covariance; Gaussian processes; MMSE & conditional expectation.
  • Trefethen–Bau → FPW — numerically robust pole placement; Riccati/Lyapunov solvers; discretization conditioning.
Stage V — Terminal seams of the original eight fields
  • Khalil → Vidyasagar — Lyapunov direct/converse; I/O & L-stability, passivity; absolute stability; comparison principles.
  • Khalil → Slotine–Li — Lyapunov design; feedback linearization; sliding mode; passivity.
  • Sontag → Zabczyk — controllability/observability; stabilization; realization — extended toward infinite dimension.
  • Sontag → Liberzon [39] — admissible controls; existence of optimal controls; maximum principle.
  • Kirk → Liberzon [39] — calculus of variations; PMP; HJB; LQR & bang-bang — Liberzon proves what Kirk motivates.
  • Arnold → Marsden–Ratiu — symplectic manifolds & Hamiltonian flows; Legendre transform; Noether → momentum maps.
  • Marsden–Ratiu → Bloch — symmetry & reduction; momentum maps; constrained variational principles.
  • Agrachev–Sachkov → Jurdjevic — orbit theorem / Chow–Rashevskii; bracket-generating conditions; PMP on manifolds.
  • Agrachev–Sachkov → Bloch — nonholonomic distributions; bracket conditions; mechanical controllability.
Stage VI — Robust & switched control (new)
  • DFT → Zhou–Doyle Essentials — SISO H∞ → MIMO; coprime factorization; model uncertainty descriptions; small-gain.
  • Hespanha → Zhou–Doyle — state-space realizations; Riccati machinery for H₂/H∞; balanced truncation.
  • DFT → Skogestad–Postlethwaite — loop shaping scaled to MIMO; singular values; uncertainty weights; performance limits. (Hespanha state-space: prose)
  • Zhou–Doyle → Zhou–Doyle–Glover — the same program at research depth: Hardy spaces, LFTs, μ-synthesis, full H∞ synthesis proofs. (RCA: prose)
  • Zhou–Doyle → Dullerud–Paganini — robustness re-posed as convex feasibility; IQC flavor; LMI synthesis. (LMIs [34]: prose)
  • Khalil → Liberzon, Switching [47] — multiple Lyapunov functions; dwell-time & average dwell-time; stability under arbitrary/constrained switching. (Hespanha: prose)
Stage VII — Adaptive & sliding mode (new)
  • Khalil → Ioannou–Sun — Lyapunov redesign; Barbălat's lemma; passivity; robustness modifications (σ-mod, e-mod). (Hespanha: prose)
  • Ioannou–Sun → Krstić–Kanellakopoulos–Kokotović — parameter adaptation laws → backstepping with tuning functions; Lyapunov constructions; overparametrization removal.
  • Ioannou–Sun → Lavretsky–Wise — MRAC theory → aerospace-grade robust MRAC; projection operators; verification & certification concerns.
  • Khalil → Shtessel et al. — Lyapunov & finite-time stability; discontinuous right-hand sides (Filippov); Khalil ch. 14 as the on-ramp.
  • Slotine–Li → Shtessel et al. — sliding surfaces; equivalent control; chattering & boundary layers (Slotine–Li ch. 7).
  • Shtessel et al. → Utkin–Guldner–Shi — HOSM/super-twisting theory → drives, power converters, mechatronic case studies.
Stage VIII — Predictive control & trajectory layer (new)
  • Kirk → Rawlings–Mayne–Diehl — receding-horizon optimal control; DP/HJB intuition; terminal cost & terminal constraint sets.
  • Boyd–Vandenberghe → RMD — constrained QP/NLP; KKT conditions; duality inside MPC solvers. (Hespanha LQR limit, Khalil Lyapunov proofs: prose)
  • Boyd–Vandenberghe → Borrelli–Bemporad–Morari — multiparametric programming; polyhedral computation; explicit MPC. (Hespanha: prose)
  • RMD → Kouvaritakis–Cannon — tube MPC; stochastic constraints; recursive feasibility under uncertainty.
  • RMD → Grüne–Pannek — NMPC stability without terminal constraints; suboptimality estimates. (Khalil: prose)
  • RMD → Garone et al. ¶ — constraint admissibility & invariant sets → reference and command governors layered on stabilized loops.
  • Franklin → Biagiotti–Melchiorri — see Stage IV; the trajectory-profiles core sits directly on the servo layer.
  • Munkres → LaValle — configuration-space topology; connectedness; quotient constructions. (Lee manifolds, Papoulis probabilistic planning: prose)
  • Kirk → Betts — optimal control problem formulations; transversality; costates ↔ NLP multipliers.
  • Nocedal–Wright → Betts — SQP & interior-point methods; sparsity exploitation; mesh refinement in direct transcription.
Stage IX — Digital, sampled-data & anti-windup (new)
  • Åström–Wittenmark CCS → Chen–Francis — sampled-data lifting; intersample behavior; H₂/H∞ sampled-data synthesis. (RCA operator theory: prose)
  • LMIs [34] → Zaccarian–Teel — anti-windup as LMI synthesis; sector-bounded saturation; regional guarantees. (Franklin saturation practice, CCS implementation: prose)
  • CCS ∥ FPW — parallel core pair of the digital shelf: sampling, z-domain design, quantization, implementation; take either, skim the other.convention, not an edge
Stage X — Networked control & delay systems (new)
  • Khalil → Fridman — Lyapunov–Krasovskii & Razumikhin; stability of functional differential equations; ISS flavor.
  • LMIs [34] → Fridman — delay-dependent LMIs; descriptor method; robustness certificates.
  • CCS → Hespanha et al. ¶ — sampled-data loops → network-induced sampling: jitter, variable delay, dropouts, quantization. (Hespanha [22], same author: prose)
  • Hespanha et al. ¶ → Heemels et al. ¶ — emulation-based design → event- and self-triggered control; ISS triggering conditions; minimum inter-event times.
  • Hespanha et al. ¶ → Schenato et al. ¶ — TCP/UDP-like information patterns; separation results; optimal control over lossy links.
  • Simon → Schenato et al. ¶ — Kalman filtering → intermittent observations; Riccati recursions with packet loss (Sinopoli threshold).
  • Fridman → Michiels–Niculescu — LKF certificates → spectral/eigenvalue methods; numerical continuation for delay systems. (Trefethen–Bau numerics: prose)
  • Papoulis → Costa–Fragoso–Marques — Markov chains; conditional expectation; mean-square stability of Markov jump linear systems. (Hespanha, LMIs: prose)
  • KKK → Krstić '09 — backstepping → PDE/transport representation of delays; predictor feedback for input delay.
  • Fridman → Krstić '09 — delay modeling; stability under input delay; compensation vs. robustness trade.
